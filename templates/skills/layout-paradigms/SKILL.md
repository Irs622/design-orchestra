# Layout paradigms

Use this skill when proposing concept directions, structuring landing-page
layouts, or breaking repetitive AI tropes such as the ubiquitous centered hero
plus three uniform cards pattern.

Great landing pages emerge from the specific nature of the product and visitor
task, not from a generic marketing template. Every concept direction must explore
an intentional structural grammar that provides meaningful visual and
organizational separation.

## The monotony trap

When left unguided, automated page generation defaults to a single clichéd
formula:

1. Centered headline with a floating decorative pill above it.
2. Two identical rounded CTA buttons ("Get Started", "Learn More").
3. A centered generic dashboard mockup or browser frame below.
4. A three-column row of identical rounded cards with generic icons.
5. An endless marquee of grayscale logos.

This pattern signals a lack of design judgment and fails to communicate product
value.

## Eight distinct layout paradigms

When presenting directions, each candidate direction must adopt a different
layout paradigm chosen from the [paradigm catalog](references/paradigm-catalog.md):

### 1. Asymmetrical split and interactive viewport
- **Structure:** 50/50 or 60/40 horizontal split. Left column anchors
  authoritative, high-contrast typography, core value proposition, and primary
  action. Right column hosts a functional interactive preview, tangible canvas,
  or live product view.
- **UX Intent:** Instant product demonstration above the fold without requiring
  the user to scroll.
- **Best Suited For:** Developer tools, creative apps, productivity platforms,
  and utility services.

### 2. Editorial masthead and marginalia
- **Structure:** Newspaper or magazine-inspired layout. Centered or left-aligned
  masthead, distinct issue or version metadata, multi-column reading rhythm,
  pull quotes, and sidebar notes (marginalia).
- **UX Intent:** High narrative credibility, editorial pacing, in-depth thought
  leadership, and intellectual gravitas.
- **Best Suited For:** Research platforms, publishing, luxury goods, and
  mission-driven organizations.

### 3. Dynamic bento system (hierarchical tiles)
- **Structure:** Modular, multi-scale grid with intentional asymmetry (e.g. 2:1,
  1:2, spanning tiles). Every tile has a distinct content role: live interactive
  widget, key metric with context, product screenshot snippet, or quick
  workflow trigger.
- **UX Intent:** Dense, scannable feature exploration where hierarchy guides the
  eye to primary capabilities first.
- **Best Suited For:** Multi-faceted SaaS, consumer hardware, and developer
  ecosystems.

### 4. Monumental typographic rhythm
- **Structure:** Restrained, text-dominant hero utilizing dramatic scale shifts
  (e.g. 96px display paired with 16px monospace sub-notes), ample white space,
  and zero floating cards or background gradients.
- **UX Intent:** Purity of message and extreme clarity. Forces visitor to engage
  with the actual core proposition and proof.
- **Best Suited For:** High-end agencies, technical protocols, architecture, and
  boutique software.

### 5. Live terminal and sandbox playground
- **Structure:** Terminal-first or interactive code/config editor occupying
  prime above-the-fold space. Copy commands, toggle parameters, and observe
  real-time output.
- **UX Intent:** Show, don't tell. Proves functionality and lowers friction for
  technical evaluation.
- **Best Suited For:** APIs, CLI tools, infrastructure, and open-source
  libraries.

### 6. Chaptered narrative stream
- **Structure:** Vertically guided or pinned progressive scroll with clear
  chapter markers ("01 / The Bottleneck", "02 / The Architecture", "03 / The
  Outcome").
- **UX Intent:** Educational conversion funnel that walks the visitor through
  problem-to-solution sequence with high context retention.
- **Best Suited For:** Paradigm-shifting products, complex B2B solutions, and
  novel hardware.

### 7. Utility-centric compact bar and control deck
- **Structure:** Dense, no-fluff utility header or persistent toolbar with
  actionable controls (search, filter, run, calculate) immediately accessible
  without marketing hype.
- **UX Intent:** Task completion in minimal clicks. Prioritizes utilitarian
  speed and pragmatic utility.
- **Best Suited For:** Internal tools, calculation engines, data platforms, and
  search indexes.

### 8. Bifurcated comparative canvas
- **Structure:** Dual-track side-by-side comparison (Before vs. After, Old Way
  vs. New Way, or Role A vs. Role B) with distinct interactive paths.
- **UX Intent:** Immediately resolves visitor objections by contrasting existing
  pain with the proposed solution.
- **Best Suited For:** Migration tools, competitive displacement products, and
  dual-audience platforms.

## Direction diversity rules

When generating concept directions:

1. **Never repeat the same paradigm across candidates:** Direction 1, Direction
   2, and Direction 3 must each employ a fundamentally different structural
   paradigm.
2. **Match paradigm to audience task:** Select paradigms that directly solve the
   communication challenge discovered during the brief.
3. **Align navigation pattern:** Each paradigm demands its own navigation
   behavior (e.g. compact utility bar, editorial masthead, pinned chapter bar,
   or no persistent navigation).
